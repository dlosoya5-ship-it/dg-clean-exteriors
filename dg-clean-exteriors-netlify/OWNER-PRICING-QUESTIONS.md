# DG Clean Exteriors — owner pricing worksheet

Use these questions in order. The goal is not to build a huge price list; it is to define a confident minimum, an acceptable range, and the situations that need a site visit.

## 1. Business direction

1. Which jobs do you most want more of: residential driveways, house washes, windows, storefronts, recurring commercial work, or something else?
2. Which jobs are not worth taking, even if they are easy to quote?
3. What is your ideal weekly mix of residential and commercial work?
4. What service area is included in your standard price, and where do you add a travel charge?

## 2. Minimums and base pricing

5. What is the minimum job price that makes it worthwhile to load up, travel, set up, and clean up?
6. For a typical one-car driveway, two-car driveway, house wash, patio, fence, and exterior-window wash: what would you be comfortable charging at the low, normal, and premium end?
7. Which services should use a package price, square-foot pricing, linear-foot pricing, pane-count pricing, or a site visit only?
8. What conditions move a job from the normal price to the premium price: heavy algae, oil, gum, height, water access, delicate surfaces, furniture moving, or something else?
9. Do you want separate prices for soft washing versus high-pressure concrete work?

## 3. Residential quote rules

10. Should the customer see a starting range online, or should every request remain “custom quote after review”?
11. Which common bundles should have a clear package price? Examples: driveway + walkway, house wash + windows, patio + fence.
12. Are there any neighborhood, first-time customer, military, senior, referral, or recurring-care discounts?
13. Do you charge extra for water access problems, multi-story homes, gated access, or a job outside normal hours?

## 4. Commercial quote rules

14. Which commercial jobs require an on-site visit before any price is sent?
15. Do you prefer one-time, monthly, quarterly, or custom recurring service plans?
16. Are after-hours work, wastewater recovery, insurance certificates, hot-water cleaning, gum removal, or grease work priced separately?
17. Who approves a commercial quote, and how long should that quote remain valid?

## 5. Estimate, agreement, and payment

18. Do you want an estimate to show one total, a good/better/best choice, or line items with optional add-ons?
19. Is a deposit required? If yes, what percentage and when is the balance due?
20. Which payment methods do you accept: card, ACH, cash, check, Zelle, or financing?
21. What cancellation or rescheduling rule should appear in the agreement?
22. What warranty or satisfaction promise are you comfortable making?
23. Who receives each new lead, and should the customer get an immediate “we got it” email or text?

## 6. The decision we need from you

After answering, give us one real recent job for each core service. Include the size, condition, final price, time on site, and what made it easy or difficult. Those examples will become the first estimate rules and owner-only market-rate guidance.
